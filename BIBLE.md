## 1. The show in one paragraph
In Maple Hollow, a small lake-and-forest town of animals, it has been summer for decades and nobody has noticed. Riot, a 20-year-old fox lifeguard, has spent years sneaking into the town's "haunted house" to finish his grandfather's old board game alone, without telling anyone. With his roommate Rocco and their younger friends Bash and Pip, a summer of game nights, dumb plans and small mysteries slowly turns into the discovery that the game is holding the whole town in one endless summer. It can only be finished together. Finishing it means autumn arrives, and with it, Pip moves away.

**The feeling:** Gravity Falls' mystery and summer adventure, Adventure Time's quiet emotional moments, American Dad's plans escalating out of control. Warm, funny, sometimes spooky, never truly creepy. After an episode you should want to play a game with your friends.

## 2. The world: Maple Hollow
- A realistic small American town on a lake, surrounded by forest. Everyone is an animal; the occasional human visitor is a joke.
- **Always summer** inside the town. Outside, the real calendar runs (it's autumn and winter out there). People can leave and come back.
- No malls or commercial hangouts. Hangouts are homes, the park, the lake, the street and around town.
- No in-town celebrations of real holidays.

### Places
| Place | What happens there |
|---|---|
| **The lake** | Riot's lifeguard tower. Where the festival game was played on the storm night. Finale location. |
| **The park** (by the lake) | What Preston wants to buy. Saving it is the town-level storyline. |
| **The forest** | Trails, Big Hal's ranger station, night walks. |
| **Main street** | Gus's garage (radio), the Mayor's office, the town notice board (easter eggs). |
| **Riot & Rocco's apartment** | Small, messy, cozy. Front stoop with **the porch light** (taken from grandpa's house). Game nights happen here. |
| **The school** | Bash and Pip go "a few days a week, because it's summer". Teacher running gag. |
| **The haunted house** | The old festival hall, boarded up for decades. Where the board game is hidden. |
| **Mrs. Brook's porch** | On the street by the lake. Iced tea, wind chimes, a lot of watching. |
| **The town line** | The sign where summer stops. Used for the road trip and the hiker. |
| **The festival stage** | Built by the lake from ep ~21 for the revived championship. |
| **The city** (road trip) | Outside, real season (cold, autumn). Where Riot was offered his place. |

### The porch light
The old porch light from grandpa's house, now on Riot and Rocco's stoop. **On = game night tonight.** Every episode ends with it clicking off, then a small Friendzy logo. In the finale it's the same click, but autumn leaves fall through its last glow.

## 3. The secret (full answer: never shown to fans before the finale)

### What happened
1. Maple Hollow loves board games; they bring generations together. Every summer the town held a **festival** built around one big game played outdoors by the lake.
2. Decades ago, **Riot's grandfather** hosted it, with his best friend and co-host **Mrs. Brook**.
3. The festival game is old and special: it **keeps the town's memories**. Its board is a map of Maple Hollow; its pieces stand for people, places and events. Each festival, when the round ends and the **last lantern** is released over the lake, that summer becomes a memory the game keeps, and the season moves on.
4. That night **a storm** stopped the game before the last lantern went up. Everyone ran. Mrs. Brook grabbed the last lantern to save it from the rain. The game and everything else were packed into the festival hall.
5. The round never finished, so the game is stuck **keeping the moment instead of the memory**. Maple Hollow has stayed in that summer ever since.
6. Grandpa didn't trap anyone. Something went wrong when the festival was interrupted.
7. **Why it was never fixed: Mrs. Brook.** That was the happiest summer of her life. She has kept the last lantern all these years and been too afraid to let the summer go. She confuses preserving memories with preserving the moment. She is not a villain; the storm is the cause.

**The theme in one line:** *"You can keep a memory. You can't keep a moment."*

**Protect the theme:** memories are not bad. The mistake is trying to keep a perfect moment from ever ending, because that stops life moving forward. Nothing in the show (Mrs. Brook included) may suggest that keeping memories is wrong.

### The rules of the magic
- **Time passes.** People age; "years" go by on the outside calendar. The season never changes.
- **Small things repeat every "year"**: the same storm on the same night, the same firefly night, the same heat wave. This is what Rocco half-notices.
- **Nobody in town notices.** It's like a gentle fog: it's just "a really long, nice summer". When the hiker says "it's November", everyone politely ignores him.
- **Rocco half-sees through the fog** because he half-remembers earlier summers. That's why he suspects early.
- **Leaving town:** outside, you feel the real season. Back in town, the feeling fades within a day, like a dream. The whole world hasn't stopped; Maple Hollow is the one place where the unfinished round holds the moment.
- **The festival hasn't been held since the storm.** The midseason championship revives it for the first time in decades.
- **The haunted house** (the old festival hall) is where everything was stored after the storm. The game's magic leaks out there: chairs arrange themselves like a board, a die falls from nowhere, footsteps move in turns, fragments of the storm night replay. It holds **echoes of a night that happened elsewhere** (at the lake). It's evidence, not the cause. Sometimes only one boy sees an echo and has to convince the others.
- **The game can't be finished alone.** It's built for several players with different views who share what they find and decide together.

### The board game
**Name: *Lamplight*** (an echo of "Porchlight"; alternatives: *Lantern Night*, *The Hollow Round*).
- **The box:** an old wooden case, hand-painted lid of the lake at dusk, grandpa's initials scratched inside.
- **The board:** a hand-painted map of Maple Hollow (lake in the middle, forest, main street, the park, the hall). The painted town quietly matches the real one, including things built after the storm. This is a late clue (the board "keeps" the town).
- **The four seats:** the board has four corners, each with a little carved symbol, and the final round needs all four filled. The symbols secretly match the four boys. They're a visual pattern for fans to notice; what they mean is only clear late.
  - **Eye: Rocco**, who notices patterns and inconsistencies.
  - **Key: Riot**, who connects the game to its history and unlocks the investigation.
  - **Footprint: Bash**, who takes action and tackles challenges.
  - **Ear: Pip**, who notices what people say, leave unsaid and reveal about themselves.
  This mapping is fixed before the symbols are planted anywhere.
- **Pieces:** small carved wooden figures.
- **Lanterns:** 12 small paper lantern markers, one per round. Eleven sit in the "released" slot over the painted lake. **The twelfth slot is empty.**
- **The die:** a worn wooden die with lantern marks instead of one pip. It's the die that "falls from nowhere" in the haunted house.
- **What happens when Riot plays alone:** he takes turns for every seat, but **by morning the pieces are always back where they stood on the storm night.** He thinks he's playing it wrong. This is the quiet image of "you can't do this alone".
- **The magic stays subtle all season**, with two big visible moments: **ep 20** (the house replays the storm night in full and the boys see the lake-side game: the twist) and **the finale** (the last lantern rises and the real lake fills with light).

### How it ends (finale)
- The four boys take the four seats. Mrs. Brook brings the last lantern she kept. The town gathers at the lake to watch and play along (the crowd round), getting together through Friendzy rooms, which is a natural app moment and not magic.
- The last lantern is released. The round ends; the summer becomes a memory. Autumn arrives, leaves turn brown and fall.
- Bittersweet: Pip's family moves somewhere fairly near. Game night carries on over Friendzy. The porch light clicks off for the last time of the season.
- Season 2 cliffhanger: decided in the story map.

## 4. The four boys
All four are boys. No romance. The group never truly falls apart. Each has several secrets revealed over the season.

### Riot 🦊, 20: the heart
- **Job:** lake lifeguard. Lives with Rocco. His family lives in town.
- **Personality:** funniest of the four and the one who starts the plans, but he makes room for the others (not a stage hog). Warm, restless, a big talker.
- **Secrets:**
  1. He turned down a place in the city (a college swim scholarship) to stay in Maple Hollow, and never told anyone.
  2. For years he has secretly snuck into the haunted house to finish grandpa's game alone.
- **What he believes at the start:** he's finishing grandpa's last round out of love and guilt, embarrassed he hasn't managed it. He has no idea it holds the summer; he learns that around ep 20.
- **Fear:** summer ending means losing the life and the friends he stayed for.
- **Mystery role:** recognises grandpa's history and connects it to the game.
- **Growth:** learns to trust his friends instead of carrying everything alone. Doing it alone was part of the problem.

### Rocco 🦝, 20: the noticer
- **Job:** "between jobs" (by choice); does odd jobs.
- **Personality:** deadpan, always snacking, half asleep, notices everything. Keeps Riot grounded.
- **Secrets:**
  1. Secretly very smart (physics books at night).
  2. The anonymous lo-fi musician on Gus's radio.
  3. Half-remembers earlier summers, and half-sees through the fog.
- **Mystery role:** notices patterns, inconsistencies and repeating "years". He suspects early, but his observations must never solve it too fast; they read as jokes or stay unshared.
- **Growth:** learns to share what he knows instead of keeping it to himself.

### Bash 🐻, 17: the push
- **Life:** goes to school a few days a week; his family is around somewhat.
- **Personality:** big energy, competitive, "the brave one", keeps the group moving.
- **Secrets:**
  1. Practises games alone every night to finally win once. He still loses.
  2. Terrified of the haunted house.
- **Mystery role:** takes on the hard challenges and keeps the group going when it stalls.
- **Growth:** persistence isn't the same as winning. Late in the season he wins one small game (not the championship), and it matters because he's stopped needing it.

### Pip 🐰, 17: the listener
- **Life:** goes to school a few days a week. Little sister Dot (8).
- **Personality:** nervous on the surface, quietly observant about people.
- **Secrets:**
  1. His nervousness is partly an act, so people leave him alone.
  2. His family is moving away when summer ends, so ending the summer means losing Pip.
- **Mystery role:** notices overlooked details about people and relationships (who's hiding what, who's sad and why).
- **Growth:** learns to speak up instead of staying quiet.

### Who notices what
Three characters notice things; their lanes stay separate:
- **Rocco:** patterns and repeats.
- **Pip:** people.
- **Dot:** weird things nobody believes. She is never right about the answer early enough for fans to solve it.

## 6. Season structure
40 episodes, 8 arcs of 5. Midseason finale at 20 (big twist), finale at 40. About 1 in 3 episodes is a break from the mystery (slice-of-life or pure comedy); there's structure, but it sometimes breaks.

| Episodes | Direction |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Normal summer life, the friendships, the first strange clues. The crew is already together in ep 1 (no newcomer). |
| 6–10 | Investigations develop; Riot's connection to the old game emerges. |
| 11–15 | The mystery becomes personal; the boys' strengths and weaknesses matter. |
| 16–20 | Haunted-house investigation builds to the twist: it keeps memories, it doesn't cause the mystery. Riot learns the game holds the summer. |
| 21–25 | The festival is revived; the championship starts; the town gets involved. |
| 26–30 | The game's history and its need for several players. |
| 31–35 | The storylines converge; the group prepares to finish the round. |
| 36–40 | The final investigation, the missing lantern, finishing the game, autumn. |

**Season goals running alongside:** the championship (from halfway), the road trip (timing open), saving the park, and the mystery, which connects them all.

**The road trip** (timing open): to the city where Riot turned down his place. It's cold and autumn outside. It turns up an old festival record or photo, and Riot's secret nearly comes out. It also brings Riot face to face with the life he chose not to have, without him regretting his friends; and the boys get to see a world that has moved on (funny and emotional). **Bash and Rocco's conflict:** Bash finds out Rocco is secretly smart and has known things all along, and feels Rocco let everyone else do the hard work. They make up before the arc ends.

## 7. Clue rules (keeping it fair but unsolvable)
Clues start in ep 1. Nobody connects them until halfway, except Rocco, who suspects.

| Tier | When | Fans may work out | Never shown yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Eps 1–19 | Something is odd about the summer; grandpa, the old game and the storm night matter; the house is "haunted" | That the game holds the summer; the four seats; the empty lantern slot; anything about Mrs. Brook's lantern |
| **2** | Eps 20–35 | The unfinished round holds the summer; it needs several players; something is missing | Who has the missing piece; that it's a lantern held by Mrs. Brook |
| **3** | Eps 36–40 | Everything | — |

**Rules:**
- Every clue is true and makes sense on a rewatch.
- Before ep 36, Mrs. Brook's lantern is never visible. Only ambiguous hints are allowed (e.g. a wind chime made of the same paper, a word she stops herself saying).
- Rocco's early hunches are played as jokes or kept to himself. Dot's are never right about the answer.
- Fan-theory decoys: the haunted house (until 20), Preston (an obvious villain who isn't) and the hiker (fans will think he's important; he's just a hiker). Each has a believable explanation for the suspicious things fans notice, and each still contributes something useful to the story.
- Decoys must never make the real solution feel arbitrary. The clues to Lamplight's true purpose are always on screen; their meaning only becomes clear in retrospect.

## 8. Tone and joke rules
- Laugh with, never at: nobody is mocked, and the target laughs along. No sarcasm, no cringe, no romance.
- Story and arcs first, random comedy second. Jokes come from characters, situations, absurd moments and writing; classic in feel, not a vlog.
- Plans escalate out of control (American Dad), but always land warm.
- **A small emotional moment in most episodes**, bigger at arc ends. They can land with no joke after them.
- **Scary:** real tense moments (lights out in the haunted house) that always end warm.
- **Tools:** the group chat on screen; the occasional stare at the camera; cold opens that grab attention in the first seconds.
- **Small breaks inside an episode are fine**; whole format-breaking episodes are not.
- **Narrator:** an older man, an outside voice, not mysterious, used as little as possible (recaps, a line to open or close a day). Not grandpa.
- **Running gags:** the hiker, the teacher's summer homework, Rocco's snacks, Bash losing (until he doesn't), Lord Puddles.

## 9. Friendzy in the show
- It's just the app they use to have fun and talk. A healthy mix of playing it and mentioning it; most viewers should know the show is from an app.
- Only real features and real game prompts (rooms with codes, Tonight's Pack, the real games; prompts from `prompts.py`). Never invented app UI, never "download now".
- An episode can be built around a long game (e.g. Mafia), but doesn't have to be.
- **Friendzy is never magic and never part of the secret.** In the finale the town gathers for the last round through Friendzy rooms, kept light.
- **The old board game and Friendzy are clearly different things:** one is grandpa's wooden box, the other is what they play every week.

## 10. Easter eggs and website
**In episodes:**
- **The lantern cipher:** a simple symbol alphabet (each letter a small lantern shape) hidden on the notice board, the board-game box, Gus's garage and the town sign. Messages are true and fun but, before ep 20, never point at the answer: side jokes, character secrets confirmed after the fact, the hiker's hiking log.
- **The storm date:** one date appears on clocks, receipts and the town sign. It's the night of the storm (revealed at 20).
- **Background changes:** the painted board quietly matches new things in town; Pip's moving boxes grow in number.
- **Rocco's music:** the lo-fi tracks on Gus's radio carry a tiny musical motif that is also Rocco's humming. Fans can spot it before the reveal; that's safe, since it's a character secret, not the mystery.

**Website** (no page to submit decoded messages):
- **Maple Hollow map** that grows: new places light up as episodes reach them.
- **Gus's Radio** pages: episode transcripts with a few hidden lantern-cipher words.
- **Unlockable season extras** (wallpapers, stickers, character cards) opened by typing a decoded word, which is what makes finding eggs exciting without a submissions page.
