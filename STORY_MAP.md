# Porchlight — Story Map

Season 1, 40 episodes in 8 arcs of 5. Written one arc at a time; the user reviews each arc before the next. Source of truth: `BIBLE.md` + `DECISIONS.md`.

Each episode lists: type, purpose, events, growth, clues, the emotional beat and the ending hook. **Rewatch notes** are for us only: what a detail secretly means. They never appear on screen.

Types: **Mystery** (moves the secret), **Slice** (slice-of-life, a break from the mystery), **Comedy** (a plan that escalates). Most episodes mix types; the label is the main one.

---

## Arc 1 (Episodes 1–5): "Summer as Usual"
**Arc goal:** make the audience love the four boys and Maple Hollow before anything is wrong. Normal summer life, the friendships, the first strange details. Nobody connects anything. Rocco half-notices; nobody listens, including Rocco himself.

**By the end of ep 5 the audience knows:** the four boys and their dynamic, the lake, the apartment and the porch light, the "haunted" house, Mrs. Brook, Preston and the park, Walt and grandpa's stories, and that someone (a fox) sneaks into the house at night. Rocco has seen Riot leave during the storm and said nothing.

**They do NOT know:** that the game exists, the four seats, the lanterns' meaning, that summer never ends, what Mrs. Brook knows.

---

### Ep 1: "Porch Light On"
**Type:** Slice + a mystery hook. **Purpose:** meet all four boys and the porch-light ritual; set the warm tone; plant the first image of the house.

- **Cold open (first 10 s):** night. A flashlight bobs up the hill to the boarded-up house. Inside, in the dark: the edge of a fox tail, a paw carefully setting something down on a table (we don't see what), footsteps going one, two, three, four... pause... then again. Shot as a quiet, careful secret, not a threat: soft night sounds, no scary sting, nothing that marks it as Riot (no whistle, no hoodie, no voice). The flashlight clicks off. Smash to the intro.
- **Day:** Riot on the lifeguard tower, blowing his whistle at a duck who isn't even swimming. Rocco on the beach, half-asleep under a towel, eating. Bash and Pip arrive from school ("it's summer, we only go Tuesdays and Thursdays"). Bash demands a rematch: he lost last game night. And the one before.
- **The plan:** Riot declares game night. He switches the porch light on at sunset ("Light's on, it's official"). This is how the whole street knows.
- **Game night (Friendzy):** a Friendzy room with a code, *Most Likely To*. The prompt "Most likely to sneak out at night" goes to Rocco unanimously. Rocco slowly turns and looks at Riot. Riot laughs a bit too loud.
- **Rocco's little line:** fanning himself, "Hot. Same heat wave as last year. Same week." Bash: "Weather does that." Everyone moves on, Rocco too.
- **Growth:** Riot is the glue (he gets Pip to play when Pip says he'll "just watch"); Bash loses again and takes it badly but funnily; Rocco is the deadpan centre.
- **Emotional beat:** after everyone leaves, Riot sits on the stoop under the porch light. He touches the old brass fitting for a second. Walt's voice in his head (a short memory line): "That light was on every game night your grandpa ever had." No joke after.
- **Hook:** the light clicks off. Far off on the hill, a tiny flashlight glow appears inside the house... then goes out.

**Clues:** footsteps in fours; "same heat wave, same week"; the porch light came from grandpa.
**Rewatch notes:** the cold-open fox is Riot playing Lamplight alone (taking turns for four seats = four footsteps). The *Most Likely To* answer is right, and Rocco knows it. The final glow is Riot again; he left after game night.

### Ep 2: "Dare House"
**Type:** Comedy + mystery. **Purpose:** establish the haunted house as the town's spooky legend (the decoy) and Bash's bravery act; first proper tense scene that ends warm.

- **Cold open:** a Friendzy *Truth or Dare* round at the lake. Bash picks dare ("obviously"). Pip reads it out: touch the door of the haunted house. Bash: "Easy." His ears go flat.
- **The legend:** on the walk up, everyone tells a different version of why it's haunted (a ghost band, a cursed bake sale, Rocco's version is just "it's old"). Nobody remembers it was the festival hall; that's the fog, played as a joke.
- **Riot tries to steer them away** a little too hard ("dares are optional, legally"). Then he slips: "Don't use the front, the back door sticks..." Everyone stares. He covers: "...is what I've heard. Legally."
- **At the door:** Bash makes a huge speech, takes 40 seconds to walk 3 metres, touches the door. It creaks open by itself. Lights-out tension. From inside: a die rolls across the floor and stops at Bash's feet. Everyone screams, Rocco included (late, flatly).
- **Warm landing:** they run all the way to the lake. Bash, panting: "I wasn't scared. I was warming up." Pip quietly stays next to him until his paws stop shaking, without saying anything. Bash notices.
- **Growth:** Bash's bravery is an act (secret seeded, not revealed). Pip's quiet kindness. Riot's protectiveness of the house.
- **Emotional beat:** Bash, quietly to Pip: "Thanks for not saying anything." Pip: "Say anything about what?"
- **Hook:** back home, Bash finds he picked up the die without thinking. It has a little lantern mark instead of one pip. He hides it in a drawer.

**Clues:** the die with a lantern mark; Riot knows the back door sticks; the town's legends all differ (nobody remembers what the house was).
**Die rules (for all writers):** from here on Bash genuinely keeps the physical die. When it later appears on the board, it is the *same* die, not a duplicate anyone can collect. The story never explains how until late; Rocco may notice the contradiction without solving it. The die must matter to the resolution, not just be a spooky prop.
**Rewatch notes:** the die is Lamplight's die, the one that "falls from nowhere" (the game's magic leaking). Riot knows the house because he's been going in for years. Bash keeps the die all season; it returns at the finale.

### Ep 3: "Summer Homework"
**Type:** Slice. **Purpose:** a break from the mystery; Pip's episode; introduce the owl teacher and Mrs. Brook; seed the move.

- **Cold open:** the owl teacher, completely sincere, assigning homework on the last day before "summer break": an essay, *My Summer*. Bash: "But it's summer now." Teacher: "Then you'll have lots to write about."
- **The escalation:** Riot and Rocco offer to "help". Riot turns it into a full documentary-style interview project of the whole street (not vlog style: they just do interviews). It gets out of hand: a bake-off of opinions, a goat (Gus) giving a 4-minute answer about radio frequencies, Bash interviewing a mailbox.
- **Mrs. Brook (first appearance):** on her porch with iced tea and wind chimes. Sweet, sharp, funny. She tells them the best summer of her life was "a long time ago, by the lake. Your grandfather made a very good—" She stops. "Lemonade. He made very good lemonade. Drink your tea, dear."
- **Rocco's line:** reading the essay title, "I wrote this. Same title. Same owl." Riot: "You were in his class?" Rocco: "...I think so." (He's 20; it's a joke and it's also not.)
- **Pip's secret seeded:** Pip's essay. We see him write a last line, then scribble it out. On screen for a moment we can read "...before we" and nothing more.
- **Growth:** Pip is the one who actually listens to every interview and writes the best essay; Riot learns nothing (yet). Dot appears briefly: she says the teacher assigns this "every summer" and nobody listens to her.
- **Emotional beat:** Pip reads his essay out loud to the boys on the stoop. It's about them. Nobody makes a joke.
- **Hook:** Pip walks home; in his family's garage, one moving box, taped shut. He turns off the garage light.

**Clues:** "Same title. Same owl."; Dot's "every summer"; Mrs. Brook stopping herself about grandpa.
**Rewatch notes:** the essay repeats every "year" (the small repeats). Mrs. Brook almost said "...a very good game". Pip's crossed line is "...before we move".

### Ep 4: "Not For Sale"
**Type:** Comedy. **Purpose:** introduce Preston, the Mayor and the save-the-park storyline; Gus's radio and the lo-fi musician; the first hiker gag.

- **Cold open:** a peacock in a very bright suit steps out of a very long car at the park. Preston: "Ladies, gentlemen, ducks. Imagine... this, but more." He unveils a drawing of the park covered in a giant building.
- **The Mayor** (lovably indecisive) says yes, no and "let's form a committee" in one sentence. The town meeting is in two weeks.
- **The plan:** Riot (it's his lake!) launches "Operation Unbuyable": make the park look so bad Preston gives up. They fake a swamp smell, bring in Bash's whole loud family as "rowdy locals", and Rocco paints a sign: *BEWARE: NATURE*.
- **It backfires:** Preston loves it. "Authentic! Rustic!" He raises his offer. The Mayor is more tempted.
- **Gus's radio** plays an anonymous lo-fi track the whole town loves. Bash: "Who even makes this?" Rocco, eating: "Some guy." He's humming it under his breath for the rest of the episode.
- **The hiker:** a human with a huge backpack walks through: "Chilly for November, huh?" Everyone smiles very hard and says "Sure is!" He leaves. Long silence. Rocco: "...Is it?"
- **Preston's beat:** alone for a second at the water's edge, Preston looks at the lake as if he's remembering something, then snaps back. Nobody sees it.
- **Emotional beat:** on the tower at sunset, Riot tells Rocco this park is the one place he's never wanted to leave. Rocco: "Then we keep it." Simple, sincere.
- **Hook:** Preston's assistant pins a notice on the town board: the park sale vote. Under it, older paper, half torn: the corner of an old poster with a lantern drawn on it.

**Clues:** the lantern on the old poster; Preston at the lake; the hiker's "November"; Rocco humming the radio track.
**Rewatch notes:** the old poster is from the last festival. Preston was there as a kid on the storm night. The hiker is right. Rocco is the musician (a character secret, safe to spot).

### Ep 5: "Storm Night"
**Type:** Mystery (arc end). **Purpose:** introduce Walt and grandpa through stories and photos; the yearly storm; the first real crack between Riot and Rocco.

- **Cold open:** dinner at Riot's family home. Walt (warm, a great storyteller) has made too much food for the four boys. Bash eats like a competition. Walt, carving: "Your grandpa used to say a summer's only good once it's over." Riot changes the subject immediately.
- **Friendzy game night at Walt's:** *Mafia*. An ordinary, cosy evening. Walt gets extremely into it (accusing everyone, including Pip's teacup), which is pure comedy. The game is a long one; it carries the middle of the episode.
- **The photos:** on the hallway wall, old pictures of grandpa at the lake: grandpa laughing, holding up a paper lantern, a crowd behind him. Pip notices Riot always slows down at that wall. Pip says nothing.
- **The storm (the evening turns):** mid-game, the sky turns. Walt: "Oh, the big summer storm. Twelfth of August, every year." Rocco: "Same night every year." Walt: "Is it? Huh." Walt's wall calendar: **12 August**, circled by nobody. Then, in the Mafia night phase ("everyone close your eyes"), the power really goes out. Real tension in the dark (thunder, a door banging, Bash grabbing Pip) until Walt finds candles and it turns warm and silly again. The lore stays to one line from Walt and a glimpse of the photos; the scene is about the evening, not exposition.
- **Mrs. Brook:** a short cutaway: across the lake, a single candle appears in her window, as it does every storm night.
- **Riot leaves early** in the storm, saying he forgot to lock the lifeguard tower. Rocco, from the window, watches Riot's flashlight go the other way: up the hill, toward the house. He doesn't tell the others. He takes his own turn in Mafia like nothing happened.
- **Growth:** Riot's secret becomes visible to one friend. Rocco chooses to keep it (his flaw, keeping what he knows to himself). Pip quietly notices Riot and the photos (his "ear").
- **Emotional beat:** before the storm, on Walt's porch, Walt to Riot: "He'd have liked your friends." Riot: "Yeah." He doesn't ask if grandpa is still alive. Neither does Walt say. Quiet.
- **Hook (arc end):** the storm passes. In the haunted house, Riot's flashlight lights the table we saw in ep 1. We still don't see what's on it, only his face: exhausted, determined. He whispers, "Again." Cut to the apartment: Rocco, awake in the dark, waiting for him to come home. The porch light clicks off.

**Clues:** "a summer's only good once it's over"; the lantern in grandpa's photo; the same storm, same night; 12 August on the calendar; Mrs. Brook's candle; Riot going to the house.
**Rewatch notes:** Walt's line is the theme in grandpa's words. The photo is the last festival, hours before the storm. The yearly storm is one of the small repeats. Mrs. Brook's candle is for grandpa and that night. "Again" is Riot's lone game resetting; he's been starting over for years.

## Arc 2 (Episodes 6–10): "The Festival Nobody Remembers"
**Arc goal:** the investigation starts for an ordinary reason (saving the park), and it leads straight to Riot's grandfather. The boys learn there used to be a festival with a big game by the lake, that it ended in a storm on 12 August decades ago, and that the town somehow stopped thinking about it. By the end, Riot's friends catch him at the house and he admits he's trying to finish grandpa's last game, but he won't let them help.

**By the end of ep 10 the audience knows:** there was a Lantern Festival with a game night by the lake; grandpa hosted the last one; a storm stopped it on 12 August; nobody in town really remembers it; Riot has a closed wooden box in the house and has been trying to finish "grandpa's game" alone; Rocco knew and kept quiet; Bash practises alone at night; the park might be saved if it's historic.

**They do NOT know:** the game's name, what the board looks like, the four seats, the lanterns' count or the empty slot, that the game holds the summer, Mrs. Brook's part.

**Rule for this arc:** the box is only ever seen **closed** (lid: the lake at dusk). Any shot of Riot "playing" is a close-up of a paw, a carved piece or the die, never the board's corners or lantern slots.

---

### Ep 6: "Town Meeting"
**Type:** Comedy. **Purpose:** the park vote; introduce Big Hal; first mention of the festival, as a throwaway that nobody can explain.

- **Cold open:** Riot rehearsing a speech to Lord Puddles the frog on the stoop. Lord Puddles leaves mid-sentence.
- **The meeting** in the old school gym. The boys' presentation ("Why The Park Is Good") collapses in order: slides upside down, Bash's family cheering at the wrong moments, Rocco asleep in the front row.
- **Big Hal** (the ranger, first appearance) stands up and saves it with a calm, simple speech about the trees and the ducks. The room goes quiet. The Mayor: "Beautiful. Let's form a committee."
- **Preston's counter:** he waves a land record. "This land has no special status. It's an empty field. It was only ever used for some old... festival." Murmurs. Nobody can say which festival. Bash: "We had a festival?" Half the room: "Did we?"
- **Mrs. Brook**, in the back row, puts her iced tea down very carefully and leaves early. Only Pip sees her go.
- **Pip's parents** appear briefly: kind, busy. Pip's mom asks the Mayor how long the vote will take, "for... planning reasons". Pip looks at the floor.
- **Result:** the vote is postponed. Big Hal to the boys: if the park was ever a festival ground, it might count as historic, and a historic park can't be sold. The boys have a mission.
- **Emotional beat:** Big Hal to Bash, who froze at the microphone: "Being scared at a microphone is normal. Getting up anyway is the brave part." Bash just nods.
- **Hook:** that night, Riot at home, staring at the porch light. Rocco: "You okay?" Riot: "Yeah. Just... a festival. Huh." He's lying a little, and Rocco can tell.

**Clues:** the park was "only ever used for some old festival"; the town can't remember it; Mrs. Brook leaves.
**Rewatch notes:** the fog in action: a whole town that once loved that festival can't place it. Mrs. Brook leaves because she's the only one who remembers. Riot knows exactly which festival.

### Ep 7: "Garage Sale"
**Type:** Slice + mystery. **Purpose:** Walt tells the festival story naturally; Riot's family history becomes the boys' lead.

- **Cold open:** Walt has decided to finally clear out the garage. He has hired four "professional movers": the boys, paid in sandwiches.
- **The clear-out** is a comedy of junk: a canoe with no bottom, Bash trying on every hat, Rocco finding a 30-year-old crossword and finishing it.
- **Festival things** surface in a dusty crate: faded bunting, a folded paper lantern frame (empty, no paper), a hand-drawn sign reading *GAME NIGHT BY THE LAKE*.
- **Walt's story** (one short scene, told while sorting): every summer the town had the Lantern Festival. Everyone played one big game by the lake, families against families, and after each round a lantern went up over the water. "Your grandpa hosted the last one. I was nine. Then the storm came, everyone ran... and somehow we just never did it again. Funny. Nobody ever decided to stop."
- **Riot** stays quiet the whole time, sorting the same box. Pip notices Riot already knew this story.
- **Choice:** Riot wants to sell the festival crate in the yard sale ("it's just junk"). The others insist it's proof for the park. Walt hands the crate to Riot: "Then it's yours."
- **Emotional beat:** Walt, alone with Riot, holding the empty lantern frame: "He always said he'd finish that night one day. Never did." Riot: "I know." Walt looks at him a beat too long.
- **Hook:** at the bottom of the crate, an envelope of photos. The top one is the storm night: the crowd at the lake, the sky turning. Someone's face at the edge is water-damaged and blurred.

**Clues:** the empty lantern frame; "nobody ever decided to stop"; "he'd finish that night one day"; the blurred face in the photo.
**Rewatch notes:** the fog explains "nobody decided". The blurred face is young Preston (revealed late). "I know" is Riot's secret: he took grandpa's unfinished night on himself.

---

### Ep 8: "The Archive"
**Type:** Comedy + mystery. **Purpose:** the boys hunt for proof that the park is historic; the records point to 12 August and something odd about the town's summers.

- **Cold open:** the town hall basement. Gus (the radio goat), self-appointed "keeper of the archive", gives them a one-minute safety briefing about paper cuts.
- **The search:** a comedy of a hopeless filing system: everything filed by Gus's mood. Bash reads every folder out loud. Pip makes a system. Rocco finds a weather log.
- **The festival records:** a program for every summer going back generations, in a neat row... and then they just stop. The last program is for a festival on **12 August**, decades ago. Its back page is water-stained; the line "hosted by" shows grandpa's name and a second name too smudged to read.
- **Rocco's find:** the weather log. He flips decades of pages. "Sunny. Sunny. Hot. Sunny." He holds it up. Everyone: "Nice." He stares at it longer than anyone.
- **Gus's tapes:** Gus has an old reel-to-reel labelled *Festival — 12 Aug*. It's mostly static, a crowd laughing, then thunder and the tape cuts out. They listen in silence.
- **The win:** the programs prove the park was the festival ground for generations. Big Hal says it's enough to make the case at the next vote. The boys celebrate.
- **Emotional beat:** Riot listens to the tape again on his own, eyes closed, hearing a laugh that might be grandpa's.
- **Hook:** Pip, putting the last program back, notices one thing: the festival's game is never named, only drawn as a small lantern. He photographs it with his phone without telling anyone.

**Clues:** 12 August on the last program; the smudged co-host; decades of sunny weather logs; the tape cutting out on thunder; the game drawn as a lantern.
**Rewatch notes:** the smudged name is Mrs. Brook (co-host). The weather log is the endless summer in plain sight, played as a joke. The lantern drawing is Lamplight's mark.

### Ep 9: "Training Montage"
**Type:** Slice + comedy. **Purpose:** a break from the mystery; Bash's episode; his family; his secret practice. One tiny eerie note.

- **Cold open:** 2 a.m. in Bash's room. Bash alone, playing a card game against himself, losing to himself. He slams the table. "Again."
- **Bash's loud family:** his little siblings beat him at everything at breakfast (cereal-pouring included). His mom: "You're the best loser in this house, sweetie." Meant kindly. It stings.
- **The plan:** Bash asks Riot to coach him for Saturday's game night. Riot (coach costume) takes it way too seriously: a training montage with lap swimming, "reaction drills" (Rocco throwing popcorn) and Pip playing a sports announcer.
- **Game night (Friendzy):** *Alias*. Bash is great, genuinely, until the last round, when he overthinks and loses by one point.
- **The die:** at home, frustrated, Bash rolls the lantern die from ep 2 on his desk for luck. It lands on the lantern. Again. Again. Every time. He puts it back in the drawer and closes it very gently.
- **Emotional beat:** Bash, sitting on the stoop afterwards: "Why does it matter so much?" Pip: "Because you keep trying." Bash: "That's not the same as winning." Pip: "Isn't it?" (Seeds Bash's arc; it doesn't resolve it.)
- **Hook:** next morning, Bash's littlest sister has written in crayon on his door: *BEST BASH*. Small and warm.

**Clues:** the die always lands on the lantern.
**Rewatch notes:** "Again" mirrors Riot's "Again" in ep 5: both boys practise alone and never get anywhere, for the same reason. The die keeps choosing the last lantern because the last round is the only one left.

### Ep 10: "Following Riot"
**Type:** Mystery (arc end). **Purpose:** the friends catch Riot at the house. Riot admits he's trying to finish grandpa's game, and refuses help. Rocco's silence comes out.

- **Cold open:** Friendzy *Never Have I Ever*: "Never have I ever kept a secret from this group." Riot taps. Rocco taps. Pip taps. Bash, after a long pause, taps. Everyone laughs it off. Nobody explains.
- **Riot is exhausted:** he falls asleep on the lifeguard tower; a duck has to wake him up. Bash and Pip decide something's wrong.
- **The plan:** Bash and Pip will follow him tonight. Rocco tries to talk them out of it, too smoothly. Pip (speaking up, a first): "You know where he goes." Rocco: "...Yeah." They all go.
- **The house at night:** real lights-out tension. Footsteps in fours upstairs. Bash, terrified, goes first anyway. They find Riot at a table with a flashlight and a **closed wooden box**, its lid painted with the lake at dusk.
- **The confession:** grandpa's last festival game was never finished. Riot has been coming here for years to finish it, for him. "Every time I think I've got it, I come back and..." He stops. He doesn't open the box.
- **Refusal:** "Let us help." Riot: "No. This one's mine. I have to do it." Not angry; scared.
- **Rocco's secret:** Bash realises Rocco knew since the storm night. "You knew and you just... watched?" Rocco: "It wasn't mine to tell." Bash goes quiet. Not a fight, a crack. (Seeds the road trip.)
- **Emotional beat:** they walk Riot home without talking. At the stoop, Pip says the first brave thing he's said all season: "You don't have to do everything by yourself, you know." Riot: "Yeah, I do." The porch light is on. Nobody plays a game.
- **Hook (arc end):** back at the house, empty now: the chairs, slowly and silently, slide into a perfect square around the table. Cut to Bash's room: the drawer is open. The die is not there. The porch light clicks off.

**Clues:** the box; "every time I think I've got it, I come back and..."; chairs forming a square; the die missing from Bash's drawer.
**Rewatch notes:** "I come back and..." = the pieces reset by morning. The square of chairs is the board's four seats. The die has gone to the board (it will be back in Bash's drawer by morning; Rocco catches this contradiction in arc 3).
