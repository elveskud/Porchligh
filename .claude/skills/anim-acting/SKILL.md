# Acting

The engine acts every line from its words (nods on "yeah", shakes on "no", shrugs on "dunno", waves on "hey", points on a forceful "you", facepalms on "seriously", question tilts on "?", thinking glances on long lines, eye darts before speaking, listeners reacting). Script tags add intent on top. Act for the thought, not the words.

## Each character's acting signature (keep them distinct, never interchangeable)
- **Riot** (fox, cocky leader): big confident gestures, chest out, smug/cocky/hype faces, points and presents, bounces when excited; vulnerable moments are rare, so they land when quiet (`whispers`, `soft`).
- **Bash** (bear, loud): yells, big arm swings, heavy hops and stomps, outrage then instant forgiveness; `yell`, `point`, `cross`, `facepalm`; when sad he goes small and still (contrast).
- **Pip** (bunny, nervous): small quick moves, hands close, sweat, `nervous`, `scared`, `embarrassed`; ears tell the truth (perk on his name, droop when sad); brave moments are slow and determined.
- **Rocco** (raccoon, deadpan snacker): almost no gestures, half-lidded `deadpan`, munches, slow `nod`; one small reaction from him is worth five from Bash. Comedy from under-reacting.

## Thought before speech
- Give a beat before an important line: a silent line `NAME (face): ...` or `[hold: 1]`, then the line. The face changes first (2-6 frames before), the voice after.
- Change the face on the thought, not mid-word: one face per line is usually right; two at most (setup → realisation).
- A question ends on a held look at the listener; an answer starts by looking back.

## Listening (half of all acting)
- Listeners turn to the speaker automatically; add `(reaction pip)` on the line whose effect matters, so the camera cuts to the listener's face.
- Use a silent line for a reaction that needs time: `PIP (embarrassed): ...` after a joke at his expense (he laughs along: laugh with, never at).
- Don't give every listener a big reaction: one reacts, the others glance.

## Emotional beats and contrast
- Build scenes in beats: calm → rising → peak (take, shout, cannonball) → release (laugh, quiet line). Never two peaks back to back.
- Big moments need setup: a whisper before a shout, stillness before a take, a quiet line after a laugh.
- Quiet lines (`whispers`/`softly`) get smaller acting, a slow push-in and desaturation automatically; use them for the heart of an episode, sparingly.

## Physical business
- Every scene needs something physical (lint warns): a prop carried, a seat, a walk to a spot, a pose (`sits on the ground`, `kneels`, `rests his chin in his paws`), a touch (`high-fives`, `puts a paw on X's shoulder`, `hugs`).
- Give hands a job: phone, cup, chips, pockets, crossed arms. Idle hands read as amateur.
- Touch is powerful: one comforting paw on a shoulder in a sad scene does more than any line.

## Avoid
- Acting every word (too many moves): one move per line, many lines with none.
- Everyone at the same energy. Vary energy per character and per beat.
- Repeating the same move twice in a scene (the engine varies laughs and stings, but scripted moves repeat exactly).
- Faces that contradict the line, unless it's the joke.
