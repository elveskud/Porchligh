# Porchlight — decisions so far

Q&A with the user, 28 Oct 2026 (moved here from Friendzy/marketing/series/porchlight). This is the source of truth until the full series bible is written. Open questions are at the bottom.

## Purpose
- A real show with its own characters, not another ad. Downloads are the end goal but stay subtle: never "download now".
- After an episode viewers should laugh, share, get curious about the app, and wait for the next one.
- Most viewers should know it's from an app, but it's fine if some don't.
- Audience: Gen Z + millennials (teens and adults), watching alone and sharing with friends.
- Success = people waiting for episodes and having favourite characters.

## Inspiration
- Gravity Falls: mystery, hidden secrets, summer adventure, characters.
- Adventure Time: emotional moments, world-building.
- American Dad: plans that escalate out of control.
- No cringe, no sarcasm. Story and arcs first, random comedy second.
- Humour tailored to Gen Z + millennials, but classic in feel (not a vlog). Jokes come from a mix of characters, situations, absurd moments and writing.
- Liked from earlier pitches, to use: the group chat on screen, the occasional stare at the camera, a season goal, the mystery, the game show, the road trip.
- Season goals: all of them together — a competition (the town festival game-night championship), a quest/trip (the road trip), saving something (the park), and the mystery.
- The game show and the road trip are arcs inside the story.

## Format
- Title: **Porchlight**. Town: **Maple Hollow**.
- Episodes 3–4 min, landscape, 2 per week: **Wednesday + Saturday**.
- Continuing story week to week. Structure exists but sometimes breaks.
- Intro sequence with a theme tune.
- Narrator: an older man, an outside voice, NOT mysterious, used as little as possible. (Pitch A's idea that the narrator is secretly grandpa was NOT adopted.)
- The look: a step more polished than the Shorts.
- Emotional moments are allowed to land with no joke after them.
- Season ends bittersweet, with a cliffhanger for season 2.
- Posted on many platforms (YouTube first).
- Shorts stay their own format (not clips) and are made by the Friendzy advertiser, not this project. They share the same characters and are set in the series' world (round 3), but carry no series hints. Timing (before/after the premiere) is open below.

## World
- A realistic small American town on a lake in a forest, with a strange secret underneath.
- Everyone is an animal; occasional human visitors as a joke.
- A haunted house (connect it to the secret). Tense and spooky is fine, never truly creepy: after an episode you should still want to play a game with friends.
- Hangouts: houses, the park, the lake, the street, around town. No malls or commercial hangouts.
- Mostly summer vibes. The real calendar is followed only outside town (the town is always summer).

## The secret (chosen ingredients; combined in round 4 below)
- The unfinished festival (the last lantern was never released; summer waits for it).
- The game that never ended (an old board game, not Friendzy; every summer is a round).
- The memory (the summer is someone's memory).

## Characters
- All four are boys. No romance. The group never truly falls apart.
- Riot 🦊 20: the main character, funniest and the heart of the group, but not a stage hog. Lives with Rocco (roommates).
- Rocco 🦝 20: unchanged (deadpan, snacker, half asleep, notices everything). Riot's roommate.
- Bash 🐻 17 and Pip 🐰 17: go to school (sometimes summer break); families are around somewhat.
- Each can have a personal secret. Episodes can rotate focus.
- New characters, a healthy mix: mysterious neighbour, family, a pet, town eccentrics (proposed: Mrs. Brook 🐢, Gus 🐐 radio, Dot 🐰, Big Hal 🐻 ranger, the Mayor 🐈, Preston 🦚 developer — not yet confirmed).

## Friendzy in the show
- It's just the app they use to have fun and communicate. A healthy mix of playing it and mentioning it.
- Real app features appear (rooms with codes, Tonight's Pack, etc.).
- An episode can be built around a long game (e.g. Mafia), but doesn't have to be built around one game.

## Easter eggs
- Coded messages and clues in the background.
- Fans must NOT be able to solve the secret before the finale.
- No viewer polls that influence the story.

## Process
- 40 episodes. Step 1: a story map of all 40 (one paragraph each) for the user to approve. Step 2: full scripts in batches of 5.
- Blender gets a 1–2 week build first (new sets, characters, polished summer look).

## ChatGPT review — agreed plot changes (28 Oct 2026)
These refine and connect the decisions above. Where they're more specific, they win.

### 1. The central mystery has one cause and effect
It combines Grandpa's unfinished festival, the old board game that never ended, and the town's shared memories:
1. Maple Hollow loves board games. Playing them brings the town together across generations; the annual festival is built around this tradition.
2. Riot's grandfather hosted the festival game decades ago. A storm interrupted it before the final lantern was released.
3. The board game preserves the town's shared memories. Its board represents Maple Hollow; its pieces represent people, places and events.
4. The final round is still unfinished. Because the storm interrupted it, the game holds the town in an endless summer.
5. Riot investigates. He knows his grandfather's history but at first doesn't understand what the game really is, or that it needs multiple players.
6. The game was designed to be played together: different players bring different perspectives, share discoveries and decide together. One person cannot finish the final round alone.
7. The boys finish the game together. The final lantern is released, the round ends, and autumn arrives.
- Grandpa did NOT deliberately trap the town. Something went wrong when the festival was interrupted.

### 2. Riot's investigation is personal
- He has secretly tried to finish his grandfather's game alone for years.
- He feels responsible for solving it, and is embarrassed he hasn't.
- He fears that summer ending means losing the life and friendships he has built.
- He turned down an opportunity far away (college or a job) to stay in Maple Hollow and never told his friends.
- **Key logic:** Riot trying to solve it alone is part of the problem. His growth is learning to trust his friends and accepting that it was never meant to be solved by one person.

### 3. The haunted house: a red herring, but not irrelevant
- At first it looks like the source of the mystery. Inside: chairs arrange themselves like a board game, a die appears from nowhere, footsteps move in turns, and the house sometimes recreates fragments of past events.
- **Episode 20 twist:** the house preserves memories of events that happened elsewhere. It isn't the original source.
- Sometimes only one boy sees a memory and has to tell the others.
- It's a source of evidence, not the explanation: the twist changes the meaning of earlier clues and redirects the investigation without making them pointless.

### 4. The four boys have complementary roles
Main tendencies, not strict limits:
| Character | Contribution to the mystery | Personal growth |
|---|---|---|
| Riot | Recognises his grandfather's history and connects it to the game | Learns to trust his friends instead of carrying everything alone |
| Rocco | Notices patterns, inconsistencies and details others miss | Learns to share what he knows instead of keeping it to himself |
| Bash | Tackles difficult challenges and keeps the group moving | Learns that persistence isn't the same as winning |
| Pip | Notices overlooked details about people and relationships | Learns to speak up instead of staying quiet |
This makes the multiplayer requirement meaningful: each one helps the group understand something the others would miss.

### 5. The storylines support one another
- The town's board-game tradition is the emotional foundation.
- The festival championship is the main public event and begins halfway through the season.
- The road trip gives new places to explore and discoveries that help the mystery.
- Saving the park is a separate town-level conflict that can intersect with the main story.
- The central mystery connects them all and drives the season to its ending.
- The boys lead the investigation and make the final decisions, but the town's participation makes completing the game possible.
- The road trip's exact timing is deliberately NOT locked.

### 6. Mrs. Brook's knowledge is saved for the end
- She's the only one who knows the whole story. She wants to preserve the happiest summer and is afraid of letting it end; she confuses preserving memories with preserving the moment.
- She does not reveal what she knows during the investigation. The boys uncover and resolve it themselves; her knowledge becomes clear only at the very end. (Exact wording and staging of her reveal: not locked.)
- She must never become an exposition machine.

### 7. Supporting characters connect logically
- Dot (Pip's sister, 8) notices odd things others overlook, but never reveals the answer early enough for the audience to solve it.
- Preston: comedic antagonist who wants to buy the park; late reveal that he attended the festival as a child on the stormy night (his personal link to the park); a small sincere moment, not a redemption arc.
- Rocco suspects early, but his observations must not solve it too quickly.
- Grandpa: whether he's alive stays uncertain; seen only in old photos and in stories from Riot's dad.

### 8. Pacing: 8 arcs of 5 (working structure, not yet an episode map)
| Episodes | Direction |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Normal summer life, the friendships, the first strange clues |
| 6–10 | Investigations develop; Riot's connection to the old game emerges |
| 11–15 | The mystery becomes personal; the boys' strengths and weaknesses matter |
| 16–20 | Haunted-house investigation builds to the twist: it preserves memories, it doesn't cause the mystery |
| 21–25 | The town championship and community involvement grow |
| 26–30 | The boys learn the game's history and its need for multiple players |
| 31–35 | The separate storylines converge; the group prepares to finish the round |
| 36–40 | The final investigation, finishing the game, the lantern, autumn arrives |
The exact events and links inside each arc still need developing.

### 9. The ending follows the mystery's rules
- The finale resolves the unfinished round through cooperation: the last lantern is released, summer ends, autumn arrives, leaves turn brown and fall.
- Final image: the porch light clicking off, then a small Friendzy logo.
- Bittersweet, with a Season 2 cliffhanger. The group never permanently falls apart; it stays a comfort show even with emotional consequences.

**The most important change:** the endless summer, the unfinished festival, the haunted house's memories, Riot's secret investigation and the need for all four boys are now parts of ONE connected story, not separate mysteries that happen to converge.
